What is the Endospore Stain?

Bacterial endospores are highly resistant dormant structures produced by certain Gram-positive rods, mainly Bacillus (aerobic) and Clostridium (anaerobic). Their tough coats make them difficult to stain with ordinary dyes.

The Schaeffer-Fulton method uses heat to drive malachite green into the spore, then a water rinse and safranin counterstain.

Results

  • Endospores → bright green
  • Vegetative cells → pink / red

Classic Steps

  1. Malachite green + heat (steam) — primary stain penetrates the spore coat
  2. Water rinse — decolorizes vegetative cells (spores retain the green)
  3. Safranin — counterstain colors the vegetative cells pink/red

Spore Position Matters

• Bacillus: usually central or subterminal spores; cell does not swell much
• Clostridium: often terminal or subterminal spores that swell the cell (“drumstick” or “tennis-racket” appearance)

Clostridium “drumstick”
Terminal spore swells the cell – classic for C. tetani & many clostridia
Clostridium morphology note In spore stains, Clostridium often shows terminal or subterminal spores that distend the cell, giving a tennis-racket / drumstick look. Bacillus spores are usually central and do not markedly swell the cell.
Look for labeled C. tetani or C. botulinum spore stains in textbooks / CDC PHIL for comparison images.
