What is the Gram Stain?

Developed by Hans Christian Gram in 1884, the Gram stain is the most important differential stain in microbiology. It divides bacteria into two major groups based on cell wall structure:

  • Gram-positive — thick peptidoglycan layer → retain crystal violet → appear purple
  • Gram-negative — thin peptidoglycan + outer membrane → lose crystal violet → take safranin → appear pink/red

The Four Classic Steps

  1. Crystal Violet (primary stain) — all cells become purple
  2. Iodine (mordant) — forms large crystal violet–iodine complexes inside the cell
  3. Alcohol / Acetone (decolorizer) — critical differential step
    • Gram+ : dehydrates thick peptidoglycan → traps the complexes → stays purple
    • Gram– : dissolves outer membrane & thin wall → complexes wash out → becomes colorless
  4. Safranin (counterstain) — colors the now-colorless Gram-negative cells pink/red

Common mistakes: over-decolorizing (Gram+ appear pink) or under-decolorizing (Gram– stay purple).
