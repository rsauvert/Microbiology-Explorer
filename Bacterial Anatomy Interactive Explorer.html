Structures & Clinical Relevance

Capsule

Antiphagocytic. Key virulence factor in S. pneumoniae, K. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, N. meningitidis. Basis of several polysaccharide vaccines.

Peptidoglycan & surface proteins

Target of β-lactams and vancomycin. Surface proteins (e.g., protein A, M protein) mediate adhesion and immune evasion.

Outer membrane, porins & LPS

LPS (lipid A) → endotoxic shock. Porins control antibiotic entry; mutation/loss contributes to resistance. Outer membrane is a major permeability barrier.

Periplasmic space

Contains hydrolytic enzymes (including β-lactamases in many Gram-negatives) and binding proteins. Important for antibiotic inactivation.

Flagella & pili

Flagella: motility, H antigen, TLR5 ligand. Pili: adhesion (UTI, intestinal pathogens) and conjugation (resistance plasmid spread).

Ribosomes (70S)

Target of aminoglycosides, tetracyclines, macrolides, chloramphenicol, linezolid — selective because eukaryotic ribosomes are 80S.

Inclusion bodies

Storage of nutrients (glycogen, poly-β-hydroxybutyrate, polyphosphate, sulfur). Visible under light microscopy; useful diagnostic clues in some species.

Mesosome (teaching note)

Classic diagrams show mesosomes as membrane invaginations linked to septum formation or respiration. Modern view: largely preparation artifacts, but still appear in many textbooks.
